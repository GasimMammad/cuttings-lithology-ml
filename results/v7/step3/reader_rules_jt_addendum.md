
## Addendum for the deeper sheets (written from the legends and the look of the column only; rules above are unchanged)

These tiles come from deeper in the same wells. Sandstone is common here and
samples are often drawn every 1-2 m, so a tile can hold 20 or more blocks.
Give every block; do not merge blocks that differ.

- **Exlog sheets (34/4-7, 34/7-4, 34/7-9, 34/7-10, 34/8-3, 34/8-3 A), one
  symbol per cell:**
  - one dot per cell: `sand`;
  - a dot ABOVE a short dash in each cell (legend "silty sandstone"): `sand`,
    and write "silty sst" in the notes with its depth;
  - short dashes stacked with no dot, or ditto-like double ticks (34/8
    sheets): `silt`;
  - a small EMPTY box drawn inside each cell, or bricks: `carb` (limestone).
    A narrow stripe 10 % wide whose cells each hold a small upright box is
    limestone;
  - a "Z" per cell or slanted bricks: `carb` (dolomite);
  - solid black cells or a solid black stripe: `coal`;
  - a small circle per cell (conglomerate): `sand`, and say so in the notes;
  - a single "/" per cell (anhydrite), "X" (igneous), "^" (chert): `other`.
- **34/4-6 (Anadrill):** as before. Wavy "~" marks are `marl`; ruled boxes
  closed at the right are `clay`; a stipple of dots with no lines is `sand`.
- **Cored intervals.** If the column is blank, or carries the word CORE or
  core boxes, use `"mask:core"`. If percentages are drawn through a cored
  interval, read them as usual.
- A stripe is still a fill only if its symbol repeats down the whole block.
  One glyph in one cell of a larger stripe is a trace.
